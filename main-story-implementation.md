### Named handoffs and optional threads

1. **Orro → shrine → Marn:** Orro speaks first, one light wakes, Marn receives news of its pulse. The first chapter ends on confirmation with Marn. Pella then points to the missing coil as the next main chapter; she is sympathetic rather than a second source of tutorial instructions.
2. **Pella → cave → fitting → Talla:** The retrieved cave coil changes a visible socket at Wammigmig and makes the road toward Wamwammigtraawsho legible. Talla receives the practical observation and asks the player to wake all three local grid branches. Orro remains available for optional technical intuition about the coil.
3. **Talla → Saima / Varo / Nemi:** Talla's completed circuit gives a measured account to carry north. Saima asks what the account misses; Varo helps test quantities before any sequence puzzle; Nemi offers an alternative reading and a clue to the late ordered activation. Saima handles the capital synthesis and the next main-story handoff.
4. **Side and mini quests:** Marn's storage/household readings become a local side quest that reframes Talla's three branches and returns the marker to her or the current main chapter. Orro can give one short relay-inspection mini quest; Pella may retain a separate household service task. Varo and Nemi can offer optional observation threads that return to Saima. Do not make any of these prerequisites for the main chapter, and do not duplicate the cave coil in the old surface-chest side quest.

### Longer term expansion around the story spine

The five chapters are the **central thread**, not the intended total amount of content. Build multiple linked missions around each chapter, using most reachable settlements, resident actors, shrines, buildings, caves, labyrinths, and cultural capitals over time. Extend the same pattern to the Human forum at Trawamprahshoaw (Mara, Oren, Iris), Boarman capital Awshowam (Garrum, Bela, Tusk), Yeti hearth (Aro, Ketu, Seli), Kobold Deep Ledger (Vek, Nix, Rul), and Greek Horde labyrinth (Iris-of-the-Turn, Ember-Tusk, Clasp-of-Seven). These places expose different consequences of the same grid and the five layers; they do not all need to be required stops on the first linear route.

Give each available resident a small authored role before adding missions mechanically: a local need, an observation or belief, a nearby actionable site, and a connection to the current chapter or another resident. Some remain witnesses or provide Intuition; many offer side quests; only a small cast drives main chapters. Attach optional arcs to a named main chapter and provide a return marker or specific onward handoff on most completions. Generate coverage reports per region (placed actors, actors with dialogue, quest givers, quest sites, unreachable/unused anchors) so expansion genuinely reaches most of the existing world without filling the journal with generic repeated jobs. Keep new authored personalities explicitly distinct from recovered legacy identities and settlement names.

### Story spine and quest taxonomy

Define one authored, versioned **main story** manifest with ID `story:concordance:opening`, title, premise, ordered chapter IDs, chapter entry and exit conditions, and a next-chapter fallback. The premise: a practical repair at Wammigmig exposes a failure in the Concordance Grid; a missing component in a nearby cave reveals that the problem extends toward the mystical Measured Oasis; competing ways of knowing must be reconciled on the journey to the central Antipion capital. This is an authored plot overlay, not a claim that the legacy save contained missions. Identify the opening's central capital explicitly as **Migbushoprahshotra / the Measured Oasis**; **Awshowam** remains the Boarman cultural capital for its later economic arc. Do not silently conflate a chapter's major town, a culture's capital, and the eventual central destination.

Every mission has `kind: "main" | "side" | "mini"`, `storyId` and `chapterId` where applicable, stable `id`, `requires`, giver or automatic start, objective IDs, turn-in rule, reward, and optional `followUpMissionIds` and `returnToMainMissionId`. A **main quest** is a chapter of the story spine and is always present in the journal once reached; a **side quest** develops a local character or consequence and can return attention to a main objective; a **mini quest** is a short, nearby activity (one target or compact cluster) that can be completed while exploring. A mini quest may belong to a side quest without becoming a main chapter. Keep authored story relationships in a separate story catalog or small manifest; chunk scenes carry only anchors and IDs, and dialogue/mission JSON remains keyed and streamed.

Start chapter one automatically on a new game, without requiring an NPC acceptance choice. Show its next goal immediately on the map/compass; the NPC conversation supplies character and stakes. Advance the story on an objective or report transaction exactly once, even across reloads. An automatic chapter should never require pressing an invisible Accept button; optional dialogue choices may affect tone or journal preference without blocking progression. Save only mission/chapter state and player navigation choices, never duplicated prose or scene content.

### Travel budget and wayfinding

Measure **walkable path distance**, counting cave corridors and return legs; straight-line distance is insufficient around buildings and walls. In the current build, spawn → Marn → shrine → the three readings → two switches → Marn is approximately **209 one-unit walkable steps**; the test-script order that touches the shrine before talking is roughly **151 steps**. These are grid path estimates, not measured seconds of play. They are a baseline to improve, not target distances for the replacement.

For each proposed chapter emit a compiler report with leg ID, start/end realm and anchor, path length in cells, portal transition, estimated walking time using actual controller speed, and cumulative steps since last dialogue/reward. Validate player reachability and marker availability at **every** stage. Design target for the first chapter: a visible first contact, one short journey to a shrine, and a nearby confirmation, with no cross-town round trip; use playtesting to tune concrete limits. The cave chapter may be longer because exploration is its lesson, but place a light/reward or character beat before and after the cave. Each town-to-town leg needs its own forward shrine frontier and clear arrival cue. On cave entry show known mission goals and all discovered exits; on exit hide cave-only exit markers, as already required by the map system. If a goal is unknown, guide to the last known entrance or informant rather than revealing the hidden target.

### Side quests, mini quests, and journal attention

Journal top level: **Main story** (chapter, current objective, next destination, progress through named chapters), **Side quests** (town/character, state), **Mini quests** (nearby and short), with completed entries collapsed. Mark every entry with its kind in text and icon, not color alone. Keep the main story visible at the top even while a side quest is tracked. New game defaults navigation to the first main objective. Selecting another quest changes only the explicit navigation selection; returning from a side quest should offer a one-tap **Continue main story** action and restore the main waypoint when there is no newer explicit selection. Preserve the player's deliberate choice to track a side quest until they finish it or change it; never silently overwrite a manually selected target mid-quest.

Link a side quest to the main story with `returnToMainMissionId` or a chapter relationship and an authored handoff line: the local result changes or clarifies a coming main decision, marks the main destination, then invites the player to resume. Aim for a return to the main marker after most side quest completions, especially at travel transitions; allow chains that deliberately hand off to another nearby side quest or mini quest. Side quests may supply insight, Lightmarks, shortcuts, or visible improvements, but required main progression must not rely on optional quests. A mini quest should usually point back to its parent side quest or current main chapter on completion. Show nearby mini quests below the main objective, limit automatic marker clutter, and distinguish **available** offers from **accepted** commitments. The map/compass should prioritize the selected objective, show the main story as a distinct secondary marker when relevant, and avoid dumping every mission target at once.
